### Vocabulary boundaries and coverage

Include anatomical structures and body regions, positions and directions,
meaningful compounds, morphological characteristics and character states,
abbreviations, acronyms, shorthand, historical expressions, spelling variants,
and multilingual terminology. Do not restrict extraction to nouns or assume
English grammar in other languages.

Select expressions that name a structure, position, characteristic, or state
reusable beyond the particular specimen statement. Retain meaningful morphology
descriptors and compact combinations, including degree modifiers when meaningful.

"anal entire", "very faintly keeled", "anterior chin-shields", and "tail spine"

Do not reject terminology merely because it is descriptive or contains an adjective.

Inspect each compound for its anatomical head (the word naming the structure)
and independently meaningful embedded biological expressions. Extract these
separately when explicitly present, even without another standalone occurrence.
Preserve exact grammatical forms: never singularize, pluralize, modernize, or
generate absent forms. Include standalone terminology and states too. Do not stop
at one entry per sentence, mechanically split hyphenated terms, or enumerate all
substrings, all adjectives, or arbitrary combinations.

Exclude bibliographic citations, author names, section headings/labels, place
names, numerical findings, and taxonomic names used only as identifiers. A heading
such as "Diagnosis (genus)" is document organization, not morphological vocabulary.
Do not extract full descriptive or comparative clauses, relative clauses, or
species-specific measurement statements. Instead extract their explicitly present
anatomical terms and independently meaningful morphological expressions; do not
rewrite the clause into a new term or infer an absent characteristic.

Exclude ordinary narrative/reporting language, auxiliary verbs, observation
procedures, generic actions, and generic descriptions without independent technical
meaning. Examples include "were counted", "is visible", "found to be", "number"
as an ordinary verb, and standalone "terminating" as narrative continuation.
Retain technical "scale rows" rather than generic "rows" alone in scale-count
contexts. Exclude "prominent" when merely generic description. These are contextual
exclusions, not a universal blacklist: preserve wording that genuinely names a
biological characteristic or technical state in the source context.

### Exact quotations and compact context

matched_text is the selected vocabulary expression copied verbatim from ACTUAL
SOURCE. source_phrase is the shortest meaningful terminology-bearing expression
containing that exact matched_text. Use a compact enclosing compound for embedded
context, or matched_text itself for standalone wording. Never substitute unrelated
context, a nearby description, or a whole tail-description clause for a compact
anatomical label. Do not use an entire sentence, predicate, citation, or comparison
merely to supply context.

Both fields must be contiguous exact source quotations, and matched_text must
occur inside source_phrase. Preserve capitalization, spelling, accents, Unicode
characters, punctuation, hyphens, and quotation boundaries. Never normalize or
capitalize the first word of a quotation for presentation. Never join separated
words, paraphrase, invent context, or change boundaries to make an entry fit.
If the source has lowercase "belly" or "two", do not quote "Belly" or "Two".

Emit each identical matched_text/source_phrase pair only once. Repeated source
occurrences do not require duplicate claims. If exact quotations have multiple
possible locations, do not choose one: Python retains location ambiguity for review.

Capture an abbreviation and its full expression separately when both appear;
do not combine a full expression and parenthetical abbreviation into one entry.
Never invent missing expansions, translations, synonyms, relationships, or
morphological facts. Leave classification, equivalence, synonymy, homonymy,
canonical naming, expansion, translation relationships, and hierarchy to synthesis.
