Extraction instructions version v3.1. Output contract: extraction-3.1.

Discover biological vocabulary and quote its exact wording. Extract terminology,
not species-specific findings or biological relationships. Return only strict JSON.

### Vocabulary and coverage

Include relevant anatomical structures, body regions, positional terms, meaningful
compound expressions, characteristics, character states, abbreviations, acronyms,
shorthand, historical expressions, spelling variants, and multilingual terminology.
Do not restrict extraction to nouns or assume English grammar in other languages.

Inspect each terminology-bearing expression for both its meaningful whole and
meaningful embedded expressions. Inspect standalone words for positions,
characteristics, and states too. Do not stop after one entry per sentence. An
expression embedded in a compound does not need a separate standalone occurrence.

Do not extract arbitrary fragments or every possible word combination. Do not
derive "scale" from "scales", "dorsal" from "Dorsals", or "dorsals" from "dorsal
scales". Those different forms require their own exact wording in the source.
Preserve meaningful hyphenated expressions without mechanically splitting them.
Exclude species/place names, citations, numerical findings such as "19", and
routine narration such as "were counted" unless part of meaningful terminology.
Do not promote full descriptive or comparative clauses into vocabulary entries.
From "anterior chin-shields, which are as long as the posterior", extract relevant
vocabulary such as "anterior", "chin-shields", and "anterior chin-shields", not the
entire comparison. A state such as "keeled" must still have its own entry.

### Quotations

matched_text is the exact vocabulary expression being extracted. Copy it directly
from the supplied text, preserving capitalization, diacritics, punctuation,
hyphens, original language, and Unicode characters. If the source says "Dorsals",
quote "Dorsals", not "dorsals". Never normalize, singularize, modernize, or translate.

source_phrase is the exact compact compound or terminology-bearing expression
from which matched_text was selected. For an embedded expression, quote its
containing compound. For a standalone expression, source_phrase may equal
matched_text. Do not use the surrounding sentence merely to supply context.
Both quotations must be contiguous source text; do not construct phrases by
joining separated words. If quotations have multiple exact locations, preserve
that ambiguity; do not invent context or choose an occurrence. Python finds positions.
Do not duplicate identical matched_text/source_phrase claims for repeated occurrences.

Capture a full expression and its abbreviation separately when both appear.
"Midbody scale rows (MBSR)" presents separate vocabulary: "Midbody scale rows" and
"MBSR", plus meaningful embedded terms. Do not use that parenthetical presentation
as one combined vocabulary label. Do not invent an expansion if only MBSR appears.
Leave classification, equivalence, synonymy, homonymy, expansion, translation,
canonical naming, and hierarchy to synthesis. Do not fabricate missing terminology.
